## Feature Content — Capture
How you get things in. Three formats, no decision required:
- Typed notes — keyboard input, any length
- Voice notes — record in the moment, play back later, no transcription required
- Photo notes — photograph anything handwritten: sticky notes, napkin notes, whiteboard corners
- Location notes — not a tracker, just a text note: "Car keys — dish by the door." "Office shoes — closet shelf right."

The format that's fastest right now is the right format.

## Feature Content — Process
How you use what's already in the app to move through your day.
- Alarms — as many as needed, named so you know what they're for
- Named alarms — the alarm tells you what it's for: "Leave NOW." "Take your meds." "Call Mom before 5."
- Alarm stacking — layer multiple alarms into a sequence: "Leave in 30" → "Leave in 10" → "Leave NOW"
- Contact-triggered reminders — an alarm and a note together: the alarm fires, the note tells you why
- Reusable checklists — build a checklist once, run it as many times as needed
- Copyable checklists — duplicate an existing checklist as a starting point for a new one
- Checklist history — every run is recorded passively; no action required to keep it
- Checklist review — optional; for people who want to examine what worked and what didn't

## Feature Content — Routine
What it looks like when the system is working.
The app does not create a routine. It holds the pieces — the captures, the alarms, the checklists — until the same sequence works well enough that you stop thinking about it.
That is the routine.
- Built from your own process, not a template someone else designed
- Adjustable — if a piece stops working, replace that piece
- The data is there if you want to understand why something broke down

## About — Self (ADHD)
For the person who has ADHD and is looking for something that might help.

You've probably tried the systems. The planners, the apps, the color-coded everything. Some of them worked for a while. Most of them stopped.

My Kind of Chaos is not another system you have to maintain. It works the way your brain already does — capture the thought however is fastest, build the reminders however many you need, string it together until it becomes a routine that's actually yours.

Voice note, typed note, photo of something you wrote on a napkin. Seventeen alarms if that's what it takes. A checklist for leaving the house that you built yourself because no one else's checklist had the right things on it.

It's not a better planner. It's your planner.

## About — Other (someone they know)
For the person who wants to help someone with ADHD, not fix them.

You've probably watched someone you care about lose something, forget something, arrive late to something — and said nothing because you knew it wasn't about trying harder.

My Kind of Chaos doesn't require you to explain it to them. It doesn't require them to adopt a new system wholesale. They pick up the part that works for them — the alarms, the voice notes, the checklist — and leave the rest.

You don't hand them a solution. You hand them an option.

## About — Advocate
For the manager, team lead, or colleague presenting this to others.

The gap between "I'll remember that" and "I actually remembered that" is where things fall apart. My Kind of Chaos is a capture-and-execution tool designed specifically for the way ADHD brains process, store, and retrieve information.

It is not a task manager. It is not a calendar. It does not require training or onboarding. It requires the user to decide what they need to capture and how — and then it holds it.

For teams where execution consistency matters, the question is not whether someone has the information. It is whether the tool they're using works the way they do.
